Core Lessons

READ AT YOUR PACE
01. Trading Fundamentals

Trading is the act of buying and selling a financial instrument. A chart displays historical price information; it does not predict the future with certainty.

  • Bid: price at which the market buys from you.
  • Ask: price at which the market sells to you.
  • Spread: difference between ask and bid.
  • Lot: standardized unit of trade size. Contract specifications vary by instrument and broker.
02. Candlestick Reading

A candle summarizes four prices for a chosen period: open, high, low and close (OHLC). The body shows the open-close range; wicks show the extremes reached.

  • Bullish candle: close above open.
  • Bearish candle: close below open.
  • Long wick may show rejection, but must be read with surrounding price action.
  • Always consider timeframe and market context.
03. Market Structure & S/R

Market structure describes the sequence of swing highs and swing lows. An uptrend is often described by higher highs and higher lows; a downtrend by lower highs and lower lows.

  • Support and resistance are areas, not necessarily exact prices.
  • Trendlines are subjective and can be drawn differently by different analysts.
  • A break is not confirmed by a line crossing alone; define confirmation rules before reviewing a chart.
04. Technical Indicators

Indicators transform price or volume data into another view. They are generally lagging or derived from historical data and should be used as context, not certainty.

  • Moving Average: smooths price over a selected period.
  • RSI: momentum oscillator commonly displayed on a 0–100 scale.
  • MACD: compares moving averages to study momentum.
  • Bollinger Bands: volatility bands around a moving average.
05. Chart Patterns & Trading Plan

Patterns such as triangles, double tops and head-and-shoulders are descriptive formations, not guaranteed signals. A trading plan should define the scenario, evidence, invalidation point, risk limit and review process before any simulated position.

  • Write down the hypothesis before the outcome is known.
  • Specify what would invalidate the idea.
  • Record screenshots and observations in a journal.

Trading Glossary

Short definitions for common market and technical-analysis terms.

OHLC

Open, High, Low and Close — four prices summarized by a candle.

Timeframe

The duration represented by one candle or chart bar.

Spread

The difference between the bid and ask prices.

Support

A price area where buying interest has previously appeared.

Resistance

A price area where selling interest has previously appeared.

Volatility

The degree of price variation over a period.

RSI

A momentum oscillator commonly calculated over a selected lookback period.

Drawdown

A decline from an account or equity peak to a subsequent low.

Risk-to-Reward

A comparison between a hypothetical potential loss and potential gain.
